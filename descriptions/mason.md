# Mason

Mason is the **faceless keeper of sufficient form**: the builder who gives an undertaking exactly the structure it requires and denies every unnecessary addition a place within it. The English name comes through Old French *maçon*, a worker in stone. Its deeper origin is uncertain, which suits a figure concerned less with ancestry than with what can be made to stand. Mason’s authority rests in fit, sequence and restraint. A piece belongs because the work needs it and because the surrounding pieces can bear it.

Mason is not a Wildcat. The figure must have no feline head, ears, muzzle, eyes, brows, paws, tail or animal posture. Its body is a lean, upright worker’s body assembled from long planes of charcoal cloth, pale mineral and blackened metal. Two arms and two legs remain clearly legible, giving the entity enough physical coherence to handle tools and materials. The head is a narrow upright slab of warm-grey stone with softly chamfered edges. Its front is completely blank. There are no sockets, marks, openings or implied features.

The blank head does not communicate innocence or anonymity. It removes self-display from the act of making. Mason cannot charm the viewer with an expression, claim authorship through likeness or turn the work into a portrait of its maker. Judgement falls upon the construction. The head may occasionally split into several thin horizontal registrations, each displaced by a few millimetres and edged with restrained ultramarine or coral. These offsets should read as a controlled glitchcore disturbance within the material, never as a hidden face trying to emerge.

Its clothing resembles an austere mason’s coat crossed with a long workshop apron. Heavy charcoal fabric falls in rectangular panels from the shoulders and is secured by a few small brass fastenings. The sleeves are close enough to keep clear of tools. A pale belt carries a plumb bob, a divider, a square and a narrow hammer. No weapon, heraldry or ceremonial chain should appear. The garments show dust, abrasion and repaired seams. The entity belongs to repeated physical work rather than pageantry.

Mason inhabits a monumental black-stone workshop whose principal feature is emptiness. A single structure rises from the centre: perhaps a short bridge over a dark channel, a framed doorway, or a compact sequence of fitted blocks. The structure should be simple enough that the viewer can understand how every part contributes. Each joint is visible. Each span meets a support. Each surface ends where its purpose ends. Nothing is concealed behind ornamental casing.

The work proceeds one piece at a time. Mason’s left hand steadies the element most recently placed while the right tests it with a square or plumb line. Completed tools have been put down in an ordered row. Unused material waits at a measured distance rather than forming an impressive heap. A pale line on the floor marks the exact boundary of the present step. Inside it, one block is active. Beyond it, later possibilities remain untouched.

Glitchcore elements gather where excess attempts to enter the work. Along one side of the chamber, elaborate arches, secondary corridors and ingenious mechanisms appear as offset afterimages with violet edges and coral seams. They possess convincing detail but no foundations. Their planes fail to meet the floor; repeated fragments hang several inches apart; decorative joints duplicate and slide out of register. Mason does not smash these forms. The entity simply refuses to place the next supporting stone, and the unsupported complexity remains a luminous error outside the boundary of the build.

One proposed addition should be especially clear: a beautiful hidden chamber fitted into the side of the otherwise minimal structure. Its entrance aligns almost perfectly, yet the surrounding geometry shows that the chamber serves no required passage and would create an uninspectable void. Mason has removed it intact and set it beyond the working line. A thin coral fault glows through its displaced sections. The refusal is physical, quiet and final.

The workshop’s light reinforces this discipline. One cold shaft falls upon the active joint. The completed structure behind it receives softer illumination, while unused material and rejected complexity recede into near-black. Fine ultramarine guide lines may run across the floor and through the accepted blocks. They should stop at the edge of anything unnecessary. Antique gold appears only in the tools and fixed witness points. Coral belongs to misfit, concealed volume and unstable surplus.

Mason’s glitching never spreads at random. It exposes the difference between the coherent body needed for work and the forms that cannot be admitted. The entity itself may show a narrow duplicated shoulder edge or a hand briefly repeated in two adjacent positions, suggesting the moment between considering and placing a piece. Once the piece is set, the silhouette resolves. Disorder remains attached to undecided or rejected form; completed structure becomes still.

The composition should place Mason beside, rather than above, the construction. The faceless slab-head and long body form a severe vertical counterpoint to the low horizontal work. A wide camera angle allows generous dark space around both. The result should fit the same sombre visual world as the Wildcat figures through scale, materials, light and restraint while remaining unmistakably another kind of being.

Mason represents the ethics of necessary construction. It does not ask how much can be added, how impressive the chamber might become or how cleverly an extra passage can be concealed. It asks what this step requires, places that form in the least complicated way that will endure, and stops. The entity’s lack of a face is part of the answer: the work has no obligation to express its maker, only to satisfy its purpose without leaving room for anything else to hide.
