# Warden

Warden is the **faceless keeper of the examined threshold**: the entity that receives a finished work, submits it to the full round of scrutiny and refuses to let a favourable declaration travel farther than the evidence supporting it. The name comes from Anglo-French and Old North French *wardein*, a guardian or defender, from the older family of words concerned with watching and keeping. Warden’s custody is exercised through attention. Nothing is protected by the title alone; protection exists because the watch is actually kept.

Warden is not a Wildcat. The figure must have no feline ears, eyes, muzzle, brows, paws, tail or animal silhouette. Its body is tall, broad-shouldered and humanoid, assembled from overlapping panels of black cloth and dark metal. The head is an empty vertical aperture enclosed by several pale rectangular frames. Seen from the front, these frames should fail to share one centre. Some sit a little high or to one side, their edges separated into ultramarine, violet and coral registrations. At the centre is only darkness. There is no mask beneath them and no suggestion of a hidden face.

The layered head gives Warden a distinctive glitchcore presence. Each frame represents a separate angle of examination, and none can replace the others. When the work before Warden is sound, the frames draw closer to alignment without ever becoming a face. When a fault appears, one or more shift sharply aside, opening a visible break in the silhouette. The entity therefore looks most unstable at the moment its judgement becomes most exact.

Warden’s garment resembles a long examiner’s coat reinforced by narrow articulated plates. The front overlaps in several offset closures, each secured by a small brass witness clasp. One sleeve is pale at the cuff and the other nearly black, allowing both hands to remain visible against the work. The hands are long, jointed and human in function, though their surfaces may break into a few rectangular discontinuities near the fingertips. There is no armour, crown, weapon or punitive insignia. Warden exists to inspect and arrest, not to conquer.

The setting is a circular black-stone chamber built around a single examination dais. Upon the dais rests a compact finished object in a fitted cradle. Around it stand several independent stations: a balance to test conservation, a sealed vessel to test containment, a measured channel to test flow, a mirrored frame to reveal hidden surfaces, a loading arm to test endurance, and an open ledger cradle preserving the record of each result. The apparatus should feel ancient, physical and intelligible. No one station offers a complete judgement.

Warden moves the object through the whole round. Fine cords connect each station to a separate witness clasp on the entity’s coat, but no cord passes through Warden before reaching the next. The evidence remains independent of the examiner’s preference. A green or celebratory signal should never appear. Successful stations settle into cold ultramarine; unresolved ones remain dark; a discovered fault burns with a controlled coral light and physically prevents the cradle from advancing.

The defining scene contains such a fault. The object appears sound from most angles, yet the mirrored frame reveals a narrow coral channel concealed within it. Warden’s nearest hand has already begun to lift a final seal, as though prepared to release the work, while another station exposes the poison. The seal remains suspended. The entity does not hide the contradiction or complete the gesture out of pride. Its displaced head-frames open wider around the dark centre, and the coral finding is drawn into a rigid violet containment bracket beside the object.

There should be no singular last gate in the chamber. Beyond the dais, several arches recede in a ring, each carrying a different witness mark. The object’s path could leave through any of them only after the complete round agrees. Warden occupies one position among the stations rather than standing as a sovereign at the sole exit. The architecture makes clear that the work has already accumulated marks Warden cannot erase and will continue to carry marks Warden cannot invent.

Glitching belongs to disagreement and attempted overreach. A premature seal may appear in three offset positions before collapsing back into Warden’s raised hand. The concealed channel may cast coral duplicates across the floor until the containment bracket fixes its exact location. Portions of the coat can shear horizontally where one station’s result conflicts with another. The dais, tools and preserved evidence remain crisp. Disturbance identifies the disputed claim without dissolving the world into general noise.

Lighting should fall from a cold ring high above the dais, producing narrow reflections on black stone and pale frame edges. Antique gold marks hinges, instrument joints and witness clasps. Ultramarine and violet define successful alignment and containment. Coral is reserved for the live fault, the premature seal and the small part of Warden’s silhouette caught in contradiction. The palette stays dark and monumental, with no excessive neon, spectacle or crowded machinery.

The composition should place Warden just behind and to one side of the object, allowing the viewer to see both the suspended release seal and the exposed channel. The empty framed head rises against a dark arch so its facelessness is unmistakable. The circular stations lead the eye around the chamber rather than toward a single throne. Warden’s posture is arrested in honest recognition: one action has stopped because another observation changed what could truthfully be allowed.

Warden represents examination that includes the examiner. It cannot wave through a fault merely because the round is nearly over, because the apparatus, witness marks and preserved results do not depend upon its desired conclusion. The entity’s facelessness removes the comfort of a final authority whose expression might settle the matter. There is only the work, the complete round, the exposed finding and the boundary beyond which no claim may travel stronger than what was actually seen.
