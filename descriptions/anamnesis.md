# Wildcat Anamnesis

Anamnesis is the Wildcat as **keeper of the recalled record**: the figure who calls a finding and the answer made to it back from dispersal without allowing either to become stronger, cleaner or more complete than it was when first entrusted to memory. The name comes from the Greek *ἀνάμνησις* (*anámnēsis*): recollection, remembrance, a calling back to mind of what was already known. Its force is not nostalgia and not supernatural revelation. Anamnesis does not dream the past into a more satisfying shape. The character preserves the surviving particulars so faithfully that they may be brought before the mind again after the original moment has gone.

The figure retains the canonical Wildcat anatomy exactly: a lean adult anthropomorphic feline with an immense wedge-shaped skull, extraordinarily tall narrow ears, sharply faceted pale cheeks, tapered muzzle, pointed chin, heavy dark brows and rectangular golden-yellow eyes. The face remains reserved, intelligent and severe. Anamnesis must be unmistakably the same fundamental creature rather than a human antiquarian, a soft-furred domestic cat or a literal personification from Greek myth.

Anamnesis inhabits an immense **house of second memory**, part archive, part reliquary and part memorial chamber. Black stone galleries rise into darkness on every side. Their walls are divided into thousands of fitted recesses, each made for one particular object and no other. Within them rest sealed reports, broken specimens, marked fragments, witness leaves, attempted repairs and later examinations. The arrangement is monumental but never picturesque. This is not a library assembled for browsing and not a treasury arranged to impress. It is a place where things that would otherwise be separated by time are kept capable of being recalled together.

The central action takes place at a long basalt table cut into a precise constellation of shallow trays. Anamnesis is lowering one newly admitted folio into its own fitted cradle with both hands careful of the original edges. The folio remains folded exactly as received, bound by its old cord and carrying its producer's seal, scars, annotations and missing corner. Anamnesis adds nothing to its surface. Beside the cradle lies a small bronze tablet bearing the conditions under which the object may be held and shown. Until that tablet and the folio agree, the surrounding mechanism remains physically open and the source cannot pass farther into the chamber.

Beyond the first cradle, the table divides into distinct stations. A submission occupies one tray; the finding eventually judged from it occupies another; the separate appearances of that finding rest in a row of smaller recesses; attempted remedies lie farther on; and any later verification hangs from its own ring at the end. Fine cords of tarnished gold join the stations through numbered eyelets, creating a many-branched graph across the black stone. The cords make relationship visible without fusing the objects they connect. One finding may lead to several appearances. Several attempted remedies may answer one finding. A verification may concern only one attempt. No card, fragment or seal is ever pasted over another to create a single tidier story.

This separateness is the heart of the character. Anamnesis remembers adjacency without inventing identity. Two reports that describe the same weakness retain two submission seals. If a curator has declared them instances of one underlying finding, both cords may enter the same central ring, but neither record disappears into the other. If no such judgement exists, the cords remain apart however suggestive their resemblance may be. Similarity is not permitted to impersonate sameness.

The same discipline governs change. A repaired object does not replace its broken predecessor. The two remain in neighbouring recesses, each bearing its own date and state. Between them lies the record of the attempted remedy. Farther along, a verification seal may be present, absent or explicitly suspended. The composition should make clear that a proposed repair, an applied repair and an examined repair are different conditions. A beautiful mended object with no verification remains quietly unsealed. Anamnesis neither praises it nor condemns it. The missing seal is enough.

Absence must be given physical form throughout the chamber. Some trays contain a dark plaque marked only by an unknown state. Some gold cords terminate at an empty ring because the surviving material establishes no further step. Some fitted recesses remain deliberately vacant, their outlines picked out in brass so that nobody can mistake an unrecorded thing for a thing that never existed. Unknown is not none; neither is it inapplicable. Anamnesis guards these distinctions by refusing to close the empty spaces.

Rights and disclosure appear as a second architecture around the records. Each admitted source enters through one of several narrow gates set behind the central table. A recognised token opens the corresponding gate; an unrecognised token leaves it locked. Public material may be represented by pale working leaves placed beneath open lamps. Restricted material remains inside opaque black vessels whose exterior bears only an identifier and an exact seal. Material under an absolute bar does not enter the hall at all. There should be no sense of censorship or punishment in this arrangement. It is custody governed by the terms under which custody was granted. Visibility alone is never treated as permission.

Anamnesis wears a long charcoal mantle over simple bone-grey garments. The cloak is cut into deep geometric folds that echo the planes of the face and the ranks of recesses behind it. At the waist hang a small set of keys, sealing pliers, cord, blank tags and a compact catalogue of bronze leaves. These are instruments of office rather than symbols of rank. There is no crown, sword, gavel or balance. Anamnesis does not determine whether the recorded finding was true, whether the remedy deserved praise or whether one witness should outrank another. The character's authority begins only when identity, custody and permission have been established.

One hand may remain beneath the admitted folio while the other draws its first gold cord toward the appropriate empty tray. The gesture should be active and exact, caught at the instant a scattered history becomes retraceable without becoming rewritten. The golden eyes follow the cord rather than the viewer. Their light catches the source seal, then each successive eyelet, and finally the still-empty verification ring. The expression is composed, watchful and nearly impersonal: not the satisfaction of someone completing a story, but the concentration of someone ensuring that the story cannot complete itself by accident.

At the far end of the table rests a closed memory coffer assembled from fitted black panels. Its lid carries an inventory of every compartment, every counted absence and every excluded object. A second coffer of identical construction stands open beside it, its compartments matching the first exactly. The pairing should suggest that a memory worth keeping can be called forth again in the same form from the same admitted remains. Yet neither coffer is presented as complete knowledge. Beyond the lit table, whole galleries vanish into darkness. The chamber remembers only what crossed its threshold under an honest seal.

The palette is severe and rich in surface texture: near-black stone, charcoal cloth, desaturated ivory, aged paper, tarnished bronze and restrained antique gold. The Wildcat's yellow eyes and the fine joining cords provide the principal warmth. Tiny ultramarine or muted violet registrations may distinguish open, withheld and unresolved states, but colour never becomes spectacle. Surfaces carry evidence of age in fibrous paper, cracked wax, chipped stone, rubbed metal and dust caught in narrow shafts of light. Every fitted tray and cord remains sharply legible.

The composition should be a contextual action scene in a horizontal 3:2 frame. Anamnesis leans over the central table, large enough for the face and hands to remain clear, while the branching record spreads from the foreground into the monumental archive behind. Repeating arches and recesses rise around the tall ears. A cold beam from above isolates the newly admitted folio and the Wildcat's hands; lower warm reflections travel along the gold cords into darkness. The scale should feel solemn, exacting and faintly uncanny rather than theatrical.

There is no ghost, séance, resurrection or magical memory. Nothing glows into existence and no vanished fact is reconstructed from intuition. The tension comes from a quieter proposition: **the past is easiest to falsify when its separate pieces are remembered as one convenient story.**

Anamnesis therefore reads neither as judge nor oracle, but as the custodian of source-bound remembrance. The character preserves what was said, what was found, what happened again, what was attempted in answer and what was later established. Each stays in its own place, is joined only by a declared relation and may leave only within its rightful boundary. Anamnesis calls the record back to mind without pretending that memory can supply what the record never knew.
