# Surveyor

Surveyor is the **faceless keeper of the grounded course**: the entity that examines the terrain before a route is chosen and refuses to draw a destination through country that has not been seen. The name entered English through Anglo-French *surveiour*, an overseer, from an Old French verb meaning to look over. The essential posture is elevated attention, but the elevation grants scope rather than prophecy. Surveyor sees relationships across a field while remaining answerable to every point from which the field was assembled.

Surveyor is not a Wildcat. The figure has no feline ears, eyes, muzzle, brows, paws, tail or animal proportions. Its body is a tall lean humanoid wrapped in a charcoal field coat that falls almost to the ground. The head is an upright oval of matte black mineral held inside a thin pale frame. The oval has no face, reflection, opening or visible depth. It resembles a deliberately preserved absence rather than a visor. Small rectangular portions of the pale frame occasionally shift sideways in violet and coral registrations, giving the silhouette a restrained glitchcore instability.

The field coat is built for movement and observation. Its dark outer layer separates into long panels that can be drawn aside from the arms. Beneath it lies a pale garment crossed by measured seams and a narrow harness for instruments. A brass divider hangs at one hip; a plumb line, sighting rod and folded plane table occupy the other. The entity carries no staff of authority. Every object exists to establish position, distance, level or relation.

Surveyor stands above a vast black relief table representing an unknown country. The surface contains ridges, channels, walls, hollows and interrupted paths, all rendered in severe geometric form. Some regions are precisely modelled in pale stone. Others end in fitted dark recesses whose emptiness is cleanly bordered in brass. The empty regions must remain visible as unobserved ground. They are never filled with decorative fog, imagined terrain or convenient continuation.

Across the table float several thin map planes. They are almost aligned but not quite: one records elevation, one the route of passage, one the places where material can bear weight, and one the hazards that would make a course unsound. Their edges carry restrained ultramarine, violet and coral separations. The visual language is glitchcore, yet the disturbance has a disciplined cause. Each plane represents a distinct account of the same field. They settle into registration only where shared reference points permit them to do so.

Surveyor’s hands hold a double compass over two fixed brass stations. One point rests upon an established origin; the other tests a proposed destination. Between them, a fine pale line attempts to cross the relief. Where the ground has been observed and can bear the route, the line becomes crisp ultramarine. Where the path would cross an unknown recess, it breaks. Where it approaches a hazard, a controlled coral offset appears. The instrument does not choose the pleasant route. It shows what each route would require and where knowledge ends.

Along the far edge of the table stand several possible course markers. One leads toward a high narrow aperture, one toward a broad but unstable bridge, and one toward a longer path built from secure stages. Surveyor may turn a small pointer toward the course best supported by the field, but the entity does not step onto it. Observation, recommendation and departure remain separate acts. The pointer is a recorded judgement, not a command imposed upon whoever travels next.

Trade must be visible in the composition. The shortest route may pass near a coral fracture. The safest may demand more stages. A direct line may require an unsupported assumption about the dark recess. Surveyor does not hide these costs behind a single preferred path. Each route carries its own measured cord and small witness seals at the points on which the recommendation depends. A severed cord immediately weakens the path it supported.

The entity’s glitches increase at the edge of knowledge. Near well-observed stations, the pale frame of the head, coat and instruments are stable. Beside an unknown region, portions of the silhouette duplicate, drop out or shift into violet afterimages, as though the figure refuses to pretend to occupy a coordinate that has not been fixed. Coral appears when desire pulls a proposed course beyond the measured field. The body resolves again when it returns to a grounded point.

The chamber around the relief table should feel immense and sparse. A high horizon aperture admits one cold shaft of light. Black columns disappear upward; thin perspective lines in the floor converge upon the fixed origin station. Antique gold belongs to reference points, instrument joints and preserved boundaries. Ultramarine marks supported course, violet marks separate layers, and coral marks hazard or overreach. No scenery should drift into generic adventure, astronomy or mystical divination.

The strongest composition places Surveyor at the near corner of the table, the featureless black oval turned toward the proposed route. The floating planes cross the torso without obscuring its non-feline silhouette. Wide negative space above the far horizon reinforces the scale of what remains unknown. The entity should seem attentive despite having no eyes; posture, instrument placement and the direction of the aligned planes provide all necessary intent.

Surveyor represents study before commitment. It looks over the whole field, records where observation is strong, preserves the dark shape of what was not established and names the cost of the available courses. Its facelessness denies the route a prophet. Nothing is true because Surveyor appears confident. The chosen line earns its place through the fixed points, visible gaps and stated trade that remain attached to it.
