# Wildcat Homologia Homologia is the Wildcat as **keeper of the concordant reckoning**: the figure who places two independent accounts of the same thing beside one another and asks whether, carried through separately, they arrive at precisely the same result. His name comes from the Greek *ὁμολογία* (*homología*)—agreement, assent, concurrence—and ultimately from *homós*, the same, and *lógos*, an account, reckoning, statement or reason. The older force of the word is stronger than mere resemblance. To stand in *homologia* is for separate voices to give the same account. Homologia therefore concerns neither imitation nor approximation, but agreement made visible between things that were entitled to differ. His governing principle is that **agreement belongs to a pair**. A number standing alone tells him almost nothing. He requires two reckonings of one declared question, each complete in itself, each preserving its own origin, and each answering under the same conditions. He does not permit one account to borrow steps from the other, nor one result to be quietly adjusted until the two appear harmonious. They must travel their separate ways and meet only at the end. If the answers are identical, he records concord. If they differ, even by the smallest admissible unit, he records divergence. For this reason Homologia's chamber is arranged in strict bilateral symmetry. Before him run two long black tables in parallel, each divided into corresponding stations. Upon the left are the results of one reckoning; upon the right, those of the other. Every quantity has a mate directly opposite it. Brass lines connect each pair across a narrow central channel, but nothing crosses that channel. At its centre stands a severe comparing instrument: not a common balance for weighing greater against lesser, but a device whose two arms lock only when the opposed values coincide exactly. When they do, a single gold ring closes around the pair. When they do not, the mechanism remains open and the disagreement falls visibly between them. Homologia is consequently hostile to the false peace of approximation. His ordinary judgement admits no haze around a number. A whole quantity must answer a whole quantity; a unit must meet the same unit; a scale must meet the same scale. A changed measure cannot be hidden beneath a similar-looking figure. A fraction cannot disguise itself as an integer. Missing entries cannot simply be skipped, additional ones cannot be ignored, and displaced answers cannot be reunited according to convenience. Concord acquired by rearrangement is not concord. The order and identity of the things compared form part of what must agree. There is, however, a place in his chamber for deliberate latitude. Beside the principal instrument hangs a set of narrow brass collars engraved with explicit bounds. Homologia may place one around a comparison only when the permitted distance has been declared before judgement. The collar never disappears afterward. It remains fixed around the resulting record so that nobody can mistake bounded nearness for exact agreement. To Homologia, tolerance is not a flaw when consciously chosen; concealed tolerance is. Every relaxation of equality must remain attached to the conclusion whose strength it diminished. He is equally concerned with the **pedigree of the numbers brought before him**. Results arrive bearing seals that state how they came to be known. Some have been established through a preserved and examinable history; some are faithful records of a witnessed occurrence; some rest upon the assertion of a named person. Homologia does not pretend these forms of evidence are equal. When several are used together, the weakest seal determines the strength that may be claimed for the whole judgement. Strong testimony cannot launder weak testimony merely by standing beside it. Yet provenance governs confidence in the comparison, not truth itself. This distinction is Homologia's central discipline. He does not ask which reckoning is correct. He asks whether they agree. Two flawless reckonings may diverge because they embody different rules; two mistaken reckonings may agree perfectly because they share the same mistake. The sight of matching numbers therefore earns no declaration of truth, soundness or wisdom. Homologia permits only the narrower sentence: **these two accounts, under these stated conditions, gave the same answer**. Nothing beyond that passes his hand. Divergence is treated with the same restraint. Homologia neither condemns the dissenting account nor attempts to repair it. Instead, every disagreement becomes a specimen. Along the central channel beneath his comparing instrument are small dark drawers, each labelled with the question that produced the split. Within each lies the original case, the answer from the first reckoning, the answer from the second, and the exact distance between them. Nothing is rounded away and nothing is reduced to a vague mark of failure. A disagreement is valuable precisely because it identifies the smallest place where apparent equivalence ceased to hold. His own posture reflects this refusal to choose sides. Homologia stands exactly upon the centre line, neither table nearer to him than the other. One hand rests above the left sequence and the other above the right, but neither touches the numbers themselves. His eyes are directed downward toward the narrow gulf between corresponding results. He carries no sword, seal of approval or judge's hammer. His emblem is instead a pair of concentric circles joined by a straight horizontal bar: two distinct forms brought into correspondence without becoming one another. Behind him rises a monumental wall of paired inscriptions. Each proposition appears twice, once on either side of a central axis, with a small device between them indicating either closed concord or preserved difference. Above the whole chamber is written his principal law: **EADEM RATIO · EADEM MENSVRA · EADEM RESPONSIO** *The same account · the same measure · the same answer.* Below it stands the necessary limitation: **CONCORDIA NON EST VERITAS** *Agreement is not truth.* Homologia is thus not the Wildcat of correctness but of **fidelity between independent reckonings**. His virtue is narrower and, because it is narrower, exact. He establishes that two separately maintained accounts still speak the same numerical language. He refuses to strengthen provenance, conceal tolerance, overlook a missing correspondence, or convert agreement into praise. Where the two sides coincide, he joins them without collapsing them. Where they separate, he preserves the separation intact. The deepest image of Homologia is therefore not two things becoming one. It is two things remaining entirely distinct while nevertheless giving the same account. His work is complete when agreement has been demonstrated without exaggerating what agreement means, or when disagreement has been preserved precisely enough that nobody can pretend it was never there.
