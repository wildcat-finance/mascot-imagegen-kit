## Wildcat Brevitas

Brevitas is the Wildcat as master of concision: the figure who insists that a statement should occupy no more space than its substance requires, but never less. His name is the Latin *brevitas*—shortness, compression, economy of expression—and carries the older rhetorical sense of brevity as a discipline rather than a mere preference for few words. True brevity does not impoverish an argument. It removes everything that can be removed while leaving untouched the particulars upon which the argument depends.

He retains the canonical Wildcat form completely: a slender anthropomorphic feline with a sharply faceted pale head, extraordinarily tall triangular ears, broad wedge-shaped skull, angular cheek planes, tapered muzzle, pointed chin, heavy black brows and rectangular golden-yellow eyes. His anatomy is entirely ordinary and coherent—one head, one torso, two arms and two legs. His expression is severe but not hostile: the concentrated look of someone deciding whether each remaining mark has earned the space it occupies.

His clothing should be the most economical possible variation on the dark ceremonial dress seen elsewhere in this world. A close-fitting ash or ivory tunic is covered by a short charcoal mantle whose folds are unusually sparse and deliberate, secured by one or two small antique-gold clasps. Nothing trails unnecessarily; nothing is decorative merely because room exists for decoration. Narrow gold edging establishes clean boundaries rather than ornament. Even his silhouette should communicate economy: tall ears, narrow shoulders, straight fall of cloth, no visual excess.

Brevitas stands at a broad black stone writing table beneath a narrow shaft of pale overhead light. Before him lies an enormous manuscript that has clearly undergone repeated reduction. Earlier pages are dense with digressions, ornamental headings and sprawling commentary; nearer to him, the same material resolves into increasingly spare leaves until only a handful of compact passages remain. These final leaves are not blank or simplistic. They retain tiny dates, figures, citations, seals, diagrams, witness marks and marginal references with almost obsessive fidelity. The surrounding prose has vanished; the things by which a statement could actually be checked remain.

One hand rests upon the final small sheet, holding it flat with long, precise fingers. The other carries a fine stylus or narrow scribal knife poised above a superfluous phrase, suggesting subtraction rather than authorship. A small brass divider, straightedge and several ruled tablets may lie nearby, giving the scene a quiet language of measure and proportion. The tools should feel ancient and scholarly rather than mechanical. He is not inventing what the document says. He is determining how little can remain while it still says exactly what it must.

The chamber around him should reinforce this principle through architecture. Vast shelves and desks recede into cathedral darkness, but unlike a great archive overflowing with accumulated knowledge, Brevitas's immediate space is unnervingly ordered. Long manuscripts have been reduced to narrow labelled folios. Elaborate headings have become single engraved plaques. Empty portions of the table are conspicuous and intentional. Fragments of discarded connective prose may gather at the outer edges of the composition, while every surviving factual token occupies a protected place near the centre. Nothing essential should appear among the scraps.

His defining visual paradox is that the smallest documents in the image are also the most information-dense. A tiny surviving page might bear a date, a number, a named witness, a diagram and a seal, while an enormous abandoned scroll beside it consists mostly of elegant but unnecessary language. Brevitas therefore must not resemble a censor, destroyer or fanatic of minimalism. He is perfectly willing to leave one awkward passage longer than the others when cutting it further would damage what it proves. One conspicuously larger preserved leaf may sit among the otherwise compact documents, untouched because its complexity is irreducible.

The composition should retain the dark monumental visual language of the Promise Machine: black stone, deep architectural perspective, restrained antique gold, pale parchment, heavy shadow and a single controlled celestial light. Brevitas himself should remain approximately the same visual scale as the other canonical Wildcat figures. The scene should feel almost monastic in its restraint.

The essential impression is not simply **shortness**, but **lossless brevity**. Brevitas removes repetition, ornament, scaffolding and needless transition with absolute confidence, then stops the instant further subtraction would weaken the truth of what remains. His virtue is knowing that the shortest possible statement and the shortest adequate statement are not necessarily the same thing.
